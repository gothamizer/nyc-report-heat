How The Hot 100 works

Ranks NYC government reports by how much the exact document is being shared in public. No editorial picks, no topic keywords.

1 Sources tracked

— — city & state watchdogs (DOI, the comptrollers, IBO), the City Council, agency report libraries, NYC Rules (proposed & adopted), and the Government Publications Portal. Report-like gov links that get shared before any listing surfaces them are adopted into the inventory automatically. The full list, with document counts, is in the footer.

2 Where we count pickup

Every link to a tracked report shared on:

  • Bluesky — posts, weighted by likes/reposts/replies
  • Reddit — posts, weighted by score/comments
  • Hacker News — stories, weighted by points/comments
  • NYC newsrooms — Gothamist, THE CITY, City & State, NY Post, NYT Metro, amNY, City Limits

Only real, clickable mentions count.

3 Score

  • 6× news links to the exact URL
  • 2× social posts sharing the link
  • log amplification of those posts (likes/reposts)
  • 2× filename pickups (cap 5)

Windows: today / 7d / 30d. Most reports score zero — that's real.

How heat is scored

A deliberately narrow, objective signal — no topic keywords and no editorial priority. It measures only whether the exact document is being surfaced publicly.

  • 6× news articles linking the exact URL
  • 2× social posts sharing the exact link
  • log amplification of those posts (likes + reposts)
  • 2× filename pickups (capped at 5)

Every counted mention is verifiable evidence — a real post or article carrying the exact link or the document's distinctive filename.

Windows

Three rolling windows are recomputed every run — today, 7d, and 30d — across the whole inventory. A document is never dropped just because it scrolled off a source's recent page.
